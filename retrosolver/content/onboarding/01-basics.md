# Unit 0-4: fundamentals

Plain prose the learner reads before any decision is graded. Visual slot names refer to abstract art the skin supplies; no asset paths, no numbers.

---

## Unit 0 — The table

### Screen 0.1 — What you are doing
`visual: table_overview`
> You sit at a table with other players. Each of you gets two cards that only you can see. Five more cards come out in the middle where everyone can see them. You build the best five-card hand you can from all seven.

### Screen 0.2 — Your two cards
`visual: hole_cards`
> The two cards in front of you are yours alone. Nobody else knows what they are. Everything else in the game is guesswork about what the others hold.

### Screen 0.3 — The shared cards
`visual: community_cards`
> The cards in the middle belong to everyone. They are the same for all players. You may use none, one, or both of your own cards with them — whatever makes the best five.

### Screen 0.4 — Nobody wins by bluffing their own cards
`visual: showdown_reveal`
> If two players stay to the end, the cards are shown and the best five-card hand takes the pot. Skill is choosing when to put money in, not hiding what you hold.

## Unit 1 — Hand rankings

Taught by **comparison**, strongest first, each screen set against the one just above it. The learner should be able to answer "which of these two is better?" by the end. No ranking table is shown as a wall.

### Screen 1.1 — Straight flush
`visual: hand_straight_flush`
> Five cards in a row, all the same suit. Almost cannot be beaten. You may never see one in a session, and that is normal.

### Screen 1.2 — Four of a kind
`visual: hand_quads`
> Four cards of the same rank. Beats everything except a straight flush.

### Screen 1.3 — Full house
`visual: hand_full_house`
> Three of one rank plus two of another. Stronger than a flush, weaker than four of a kind.

### Screen 1.4 — Flush
`visual: hand_flush`
> Five cards of the same suit, not in order. If both players have a flush, the higher cards decide it.

### Screen 1.5 — Straight
`visual: hand_straight`
> Five cards in a row, any suits. An ace can be high or low, but the row cannot wrap around.

### Screen 1.6 — Three of a kind, two pair, one pair
`visual: hand_trips_pair`
> Below the straights come three of a kind, then two pair, then one pair. Each is a small step down from the one above.

### Screen 1.7 — High card
`visual: hand_high_card`
> No pair, no row, no suit-match. Your best single card is all you have. This is the most common hand and it often loses.

### Recall 1 — Order them
`gate: recognize` — drag or tap to order five shown hands strongest to weakest. No score, no timer. A wrong order shows the correct step and lets the learner retry.

## Unit 2 — The flow of one hand

Told as the story of a single hand, start to finish. Terms are introduced on the screen where they first matter.

### Screen 2.1 — Blinds
`visual: blinds_chips`
> Before any cards are dealt, two players must put money in. These are forced bets called the blinds. They give everyone something to play for and something to win.

### Screen 2.2 — The deal
`visual: deal_cards`
> Every player gets two private cards. Before the shared cards appear, a round of betting opens. Who acts first depends on the table: at a standard multiway table (three or more players), the player immediately left of the big blind acts first preflop — the big blind is last. Heads-up is the exception: the button acts first preflop. This first round is the **preflop**.

### Screen 2.3 — The flop
`visual: flop_cards`
> Three shared cards come out at once. This is the **flop**. Now everyone can see part of their hand forming, and a round of betting follows.

### Screen 2.4 — The turn and the river
`visual: turn_river`
> One more shared card is the **turn**, then a final one is the **river**. A betting round follows each. After the river, no more cards come out.

### Screen 2.5 — Folding, calling, raising
`visual: action_ring`
> On your turn you may **fold** (give up the hand and any money already in), **call** (match the current bet), or **raise** (increase it). Every choice costs something, including choosing nothing.

### Screen 2.6 — The showdown
`visual: showdown_reveal`
> If more than one player is still in after the last bet, the remaining cards are shown. The best five-card hand takes the pot.

### Recall 2 — Sequence it
`gate: recognize` — order the stages: blinds, deal, flop, turn, river, showdown. Retry allowed.

## Unit 3 — Positions

### Screen 3.1 — Seat order matters
`visual: seat_ring`
> Players act in a fixed order around the table. Where you sit decides when you act on every round, and acting later means acting with more information.

### Screen 3.2 — Early and late
`visual: seat_ring`
> The players who act first are **early**. The players who act last are **late**. Late is generally an easier place to be, because you have already seen what the others did.

### Screen 3.3 — The button
`visual: button_marker`
> A marker called the **button** moves one seat each hand. The button acts last after the shared cards come, marking the best seat for that hand. It also rotates, so over a session no one keeps the same seat.

### Screen 3.4 — In position and out of position
`visual: seat_ring`
> When you act after your opponent you are **in position**. When you act before them you are **out of position**. Out of position you must commit to a choice without knowing what they will do.

### Recall 3 — Who acts last?
`gate: recognize` — given a seat marker and the button, tap the seat that acts last. No score.

## Unit 4 — What a "spot" is

### Screen 4.1 — A spot
`visual: spot_frame`
> A **spot** is one decision point: your cards, the shared cards, the money already in, and what has happened so far. Everything up to that moment is the situation. Your choice is the decision.

### Screen 4.2 — Spots repeat
`visual: spot_frame`
> The same situation comes up again and again with different cards. That is why practice works: you learn the shape of a situation, not one lucky deal.

### Screen 4.3 — Grading
`visual: grade_meter`
> When you practise a spot, your choice is graded against the best choice for that spot. A good choice may still lose the hand — grading is about the decision, not the deal.

### Screen 4.4 — The teach card
`visual: teach_card`
> After a miss, a teach card explains why the better choice was better. It is the lesson, and it is never hidden. The skin may dress it up, but the explanation always appears.

### Recall 4 — Name the parts
`gate: recognize` — tap each part of a shown spot: your cards, the shared cards, the pot, the decision. Retry allowed.

## Where practice begins

After Unit 4, the learner moves to **Lesson 1**. From there, screens with `gate: solve` are graded on real, engine-supplied spots. Units 0-4 are never graded, so a beginner cannot fail before they have been taught anything.
